The invoice is the product

Nov 20, 2026

Every case of wine that comes through the door arrives with a piece of paper. Bottle count, case price, deposit, discounts that may or may not have been applied. Nobody opens a shop because they love that piece of paper.

But that’s where the margin lives. A mis-keyed cost turns a good bottle into a loss leader and you don’t find out until the quarter is over.

So the first real thing I built wasn’t a pretty storefront feature. It was intake: photograph the invoice, check every line against what the bottle should cost, receive it into inventory, and flag what doesn’t match. Boring. Enormously useful.

Automate the thing you dread, not the thing that’s fun.

The fun things you’ll do anyway. The dreaded things are where errors hide, because a tired person at 6pm is the least reliable system in any business. Give that job to something that doesn’t get tired, and spend the reclaimed hour on the floor, talking to someone about what they’re cooking tonight.
